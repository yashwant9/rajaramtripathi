01The Beginning

Rooted in Bastar.
Driven by Purpose.

The journey began in the forests of Bastar, where farming was deeply connected to nature, tradition, and community life.

Observing these systems closely revealed a powerful truth — that agriculture, when done right, can sustain not just land, but livelihoods and dignity.

This understanding became the foundation of a lifelong mission: to create farming models that are economically viable, environmentally sustainable, and scalable for the future.

01

Environment & Biodiversity

A strong advocate of nature-led farming, Dr. Tripathi believes that environmental protection is only possible when natural ecosystems are allowed to grow and function organically.

  • Conserved 250+ rare medicinal plant species, many listed in the United Nations Red Data Book
  • Planted 3.4 lakh+ trees, contributing significantly to ecological restoration
  • Developed the Ethnico Medico Park, a living herbal forest preserving endangered species
02

Farming Innovation & Approach

Dr. Tripathi has redefined conventional agricultural practices by introducing scientific and ecological methods into farming. His approach challenges traditional assumptions — for instance, even termites, typically seen as harmful, are integrated into the ecosystem for their role in improving soil nutrition. These innovations demonstrate how natural processes can be leveraged rather than controlled.

04The Working Model

A working model of sustainable farming

Developed over decades, this model integrates biodiversity, scientific methods, and market linkage — demonstrating how farming can be both sustainable and profitable.

Under the supervision of Tripathi, 34 acres of land in Kondagaon is being cultivated extensively by certified organic method, along with black pepper and bitter stevia cultivated on a large scale. To extend this model, sustainable organic farming is being done on 1,100 acres of land in the Bastar region with several farmer groups. Biodiversity is clearly reflected in Maa Danteshwari Herbal Farm — a special farm of its kind in the country.

In the villages of the Bastar region — Salna, Palna, Marangpuri, Randhana, Makdi, Chikalputi, Sambalpur, Chapka, Poleng, and Karanji — organic farming and vegetable cultivation is being done on 700 acres with the help of farmer groups. In the farm, 3.4 lakh trees and more than 240 kinds of medicinal herbs, spices, and high-value crops are cultivated through the crop cycle, with pests managed by biological technology.
